# Figure legends

## Figure 1 | Single-cell eQTL-guided translational prioritization in PBC

Overview of the staged evidence workflow. The discovery screen integrated 56 primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) loci with 17 OneK1K immune-cell populations and identified 152 locus–gene–cell records with credible-set overlap. Signal resolution used local signed linkage disequilibrium, SuSiE fine-mapping and signal-level colocalization. External validation comprised independent molecular-QTL studies, FinnGen and UK Biobank disease associations, liver donor-level single-cell analyses, bulk transcriptomics and proteomic audits. The translation gate integrated allele direction, TYK2 functional-variant evidence, drug and clinical-trial evidence, safety, and negative findings. Counts represent screened records or tested combinations and should not be interpreted as independent discoveries. The final core unit was the IL12RB2–TYK2 axis; deucravacitinib was classified as CONDITIONAL GO for experimental evaluation, whereas direct IL12RB2 small-molecule docking was classified as NO-GO.

## Figure 2 | PBC GWAS anchoring and fine-mapping of the IL12RB2 locus

**A,** Chromosome-colored Manhattan plot of the PBC discovery GWAS (GCST90061440). The lower rug marks genome-wide significant variants; annotations are restricted to IL12RB2/rs6679356, TYK2/rs2304256, the HLA region and selected independent peaks. The dashed line marks P=5×10^-8. TYK2 is shown as a genome-wide disease-locus anchor and is not implied to have the same cell-specific colocalization evidence as IL12RB2. **B,** LocusZoom-style regional association plot at 1p31.3. Points are colored by linkage disequilibrium (r²) with rs6679356 using the 1000 Genomes European reference panel; enlarged colored points denote members of the three PBC 95% credible sets, and the lower track shows regional gene positions. **C,** Conditional inclusion probabilities of variants in the three 95% credible sets from the L=10 `susie_rss` model. **D,** Variant-level trajectories of the first three PBC SuSiE components, showing distinct signal-top variants rs6679356, rs3828069 and rs112588573. The summary-statistic model converged and included 3,638 aligned variants. Fine-mapping depends on the external signed-LD reference.

## Figure 3 | Discovery colocalization and external validation of IL12RB2

**A,** Discovery signal-pair `coloc.susie` analysis for three PBC and three IL12RB2/NK-cell eQTL components, with a separate prior-sensitivity plot for the prespecified signal-1 pair. PBC signal 1 shared rs6679356 with the primary eQTL component (PP.H4=0.971), and PP.H4 remained 0.971–0.997 across the tested p12 range. The convergent L=3 eQTL review was used because the L=10 model did not converge. **B,** External signal-level colocalization with Perez NK, GTEx whole blood and Nathan Th1 eQTL data. Perez NK and GTEx whole blood strongly replicated PBC signal 1, whereas Nathan Th1 did not replicate the principal signal and its best pairing was exploratory for PBC signal 3. **C,** Allele-aligned effects of rs6679356-T in the discovery PBC GWAS, FinnGen R12 and UK Biobank, together with the FinnGen variant, regional and MAGMA-compatible gene-level evidence ladder. The IL12RB2 gene result was FDR-significant but not Bonferroni-significant. **D,** FinnGen regional Wakefield approximate-Bayes-factor analysis and FinnGen–eQTL BF–BF prior sensitivity. Perez NK and GTEx whole blood were regionally compatible, whereas Nathan Th1 was unsupported. The BF–BF analysis is a single-causal approximation, not formal FinnGen `coloc.susie`, because complete unthresholded signed Finnish LD was unavailable. **E,** Allele-aligned disease, RNA and protein effects for rs6679356-T. The allele lowered PBC risk and IL12RB2 RNA, whereas its cerebrospinal-fluid protein-QTL effect was directionally discordant and compartment-specific. This panel does not establish a formal mediation chain.

## Figure 4 | Cross-platform protein boundary and TYK2 human genetic evidence

**A,** Allele-aligned IL12RB2 cis-pQTL effects for the Olink lead, SomaScan lead and PBC lead rs6679356 in the same 1,889 MESA participants. Error bars are 95% confidence intervals. Olink and SomaScan values are assay-scale effects and should not be compared as if measured on a common quantitative scale. **B,** Comparison of Olink and SomaScan z scores across 8,647 allele-aligned IL12RB2 cis variants. Only 47.45% had concordant directions; Pearson correlation was −0.084, and single-causal ABF sensitivity yielded PP.H4≤0.0166. These results do not support cross-platform replication of one circulating IL12RB2 signal and may reflect epitope, fragment, protein-altering-variant, post-translational or assay effects. **C,** Fixed-effect inverse-variance meta-analysis of the TYK2 partial loss-of-function rs34536443-C allele in FinnGen and UKB–TOPMed (1,016 cases and 770,550 controls; meta-analysis OR=0.560, 95% CI 0.432–0.727; P=1.31×10^-5; I²=0%). **D,** Marginal and mutually conditioned PBC z scores for rs34536443 and rs2304256 using 1000 Genomes European signed dosage correlation. Both retained evidence after approximate conditioning. This summary-z calculation is not individual-level COJO. **E,** Signed-LD distributions from 100,000 bootstrap draws in the full European panel, Finnish subset and non-Finnish European subset. Both conditional associations remained nominally significant in essentially all valid draws. Finnish rare-allele monomorphic draws were excluded, and the Finnish 1000 Genomes subset is not FinnGen study-specific signed LD.

## Figure 5 | Liver single-cell localization of the IL12RB2–TYK2–STAT4–IFNG axis

**A,** Donor-aware UMAP of 69,436 quality-controlled, SoupX-corrected liver cells from HRA008003 (five PBC and five control donors). Target-independent SingleR annotation resolved parenchymal, stromal and immune compartments, with T/NK candidates refined into CD4 T, CD8 T, liver-resident NK (lrNK) and conventional/circulating NK (cNK) states. fastMNN correction used donor/sample as the batch variable; the embedding is used for visualization only. **B,** Dot plot of canonical markers supporting the SingleR-derived labels. Marker size denotes the fraction of expressing cells and color denotes within-gene scaled mean expression. IL12RB2, TYK2, STAT4 and IFNG were excluded from annotation training to avoid circularity. **C,** IL12RB2 feature UMAP and T/NK target-axis dot plot. IL12RB2 was sparse at single-cell resolution (1.6% positive overall), whereas TYK2, STAT4 and IFNG showed broader but context-dependent expression. Cell-level displays are descriptive and are not treated as independent replicates. **D,** Per-donor lrNK fraction among refined NK cells. Strong donor heterogeneity was observed, but PBC and control groups did not differ in the exact two-sided donor-label permutation test (P=0.508; 252 assignments). **E,** edgeR robust quasi-likelihood estimates from donor-level pseudobulk contrasts for IL12RB2, TYK2, STAT4 and IFNG across CD4 T, cNK, lrNK and CD8 T compartments. IL12RB2 was directionally increased in all four compartments and was strongest in CD4 T (log2 fold change=2.006, nominal P=0.022) and cNK (log2 fold change=0.901, P=0.070), but neither effect passed transcriptome-wide FDR. CD8 T STAT4 was decreased and passed transcriptome-wide FDR (FDR=0.043), whereas the other displayed target effects are exploratory. **F,** IL12RB2 robust quasi-likelihood effect estimates annotated with exact donor-label permutation P values and leave-one-donor-out (LODO) direction preservation. All four compartment-level IL12RB2 effects preserved their positive direction in all ten donor-deletion analyses; this stability does not constitute independent external replication. All disease and composition tests use donors, rather than cells, as the independent units.

## Figure 6 | Independent single-cell resource audit and meta-validation

**A,** Independent within-patient antigen-context analysis in GSE270739. IL12RB2 expression was compared between PDC-E2-reactive and pooled foreign-antigen-reactive circulating memory CD4 T cells after repeat visits were collapsed to five independent pure-PBC patients. The mean paired pseudobulk difference was 2.222 log2(CPM+0.5) units (95% CI, -2.891 to 7.336; exact two-sided sign-flip P=0.500), with two of five patients showing a positive direction. This analysis tests antigen context within PBC and is not a PBC-versus-healthy comparison. **B,** Donor-paired lrNK-minus-cNK IL12RB2 logCPM contrasts in three independent human liver single-cell cohorts. The J Hepatology atlas, HRA002347 5-prime arm and HRA008003 each had a positive mean contrast. HRA002347 3-prime data were excluded from independent cohort counting because they profile the same nine donors as the 5-prime arm. **C,** Fixed-effect and Hartung-Knapp random-effects meta-analysis of the three independent cohort estimates. All three effects were positive, but the Hartung-Knapp estimate remained imprecise (random-effects estimate=1.723; 95% CI, -1.637 to 5.084; one-sided P=0.079; I2=98.4%; tau2=2.536). The result is therefore classified as directionally consistent but highly heterogeneous, rather than definitive independent replication. **D,** Systematic audit of public PBC single-cell resources through 16 October 2026. Eligibility dimensions included relevant human PBC liver tissue, donor identifiers, reusable cell- or sample-level matrices, donor independence, measurable IL12RB2 and a valid donor-level PBC-control contrast. No fourth eligible public donor-level PBC-control single-cell cohort was identified. Overlapping donor frameworks, antigen/condition-level technical units and studies lacking a reusable public matrix are shown explicitly.

## Figure 7 | Orthogonal molecular evidence and a guarded translational path for the IL12RB2–TYK2 axis

**A,** Modified Hartung–Knapp random-effects meta-analysis of prespecified IL12–STAT4 and IFNG-response robust-z pathway scores across four independent PBC transcriptomic cohorts. Both pooled effects were positive and all four cohort-specific effects had the same direction (IL12–STAT4 estimate=0.930, 95% CI, -1.073 to 2.933, P=0.236, FDR=0.315, I2=71.1%; IFNG response estimate=0.765, 95% CI, -0.320 to 1.849, P=0.111, FDR=0.221, I2=42.8%). Direction was preserved under rank-based scoring, leave-one-cohort-out and leave-one-gene-out analyses. These results are classified as cross-cohort directional pathway support, not statistically significant transcriptomic replication. **B,** PXD083842 liver-proteomic TYK2 effects for PBC versus healthy controls (14 versus 9 samples) across seven robust statistical methods. The unadjusted increase was directionally consistent (approximately 0.40–0.43 log2 units; Welch P=0.00513; permutation P=0.00094; OLS HC3 P=0.00124; Huber P=0.000526) and retained its direction in all leave-one-sample-out analyses. Adjustment-status tiles show whether TYK2 appeared in the authors' released FDR<0.05 lists. It remained listed in the unadjusted and cirrhosis-adjusted analyses but not after age, sex, BMI or age-plus-sex adjustment, exclusion of concurrent MASLD, or comparison with MASLD. Age had minimal overlap between groups, 43% of PBC liver samples had concurrent MASLD, and recruitment stream was completely confounded with disease. Because the public supplement reports only significant adjusted proteins, exact non-significant TYK2 coefficients are unavailable; the result is supportive and outlier-robust but not an independent, disease-specific protein replication. **C,** Evidence-availability matrix integrating Open Targets and ChEMBL target/modality records, ClinicalTrials.gov PBC studies, regulatory-label safety information and genetic-direction matching. Database presence denotes an available evidence type rather than PBC efficacy. No TYK2-directed PBC trial was identified. In NCT01389973, none of 20 ustekinumab-treated participants met the week-12 alkaline-phosphatase response; NCT03742973 evaluated baricitinib but terminated after two participants because of enrollment futility. **D,** Prespecified traffic-light gates for selective TYK2 inhibition and direct IL12RB2 small-molecule development. Deucravacitinib passes the mechanistic-direction and modality-availability gates but lacks direct PBC efficacy data and requires liver-enzyme, serious-infection and latent-tuberculosis monitoring; severe hepatic impairment is a red gate because use in Child–Pugh C is not recommended. It is therefore classified as CONDITIONAL GO for preclinical or early proof-of-mechanism evaluation only. IL12RB2 has experimental receptor structures (PDB 8XRP and 8YI7), but no validated ligand-bound small-molecule pocket, ChEMBL activity record or docking benchmark was identified; direct small-molecule docking is classified as NO-GO. **E,** Final claim hierarchy. Genetic target support for the IL12RB2–TYK2 axis is graded moderate-to-strong, the translational drug hypothesis is conditional, and a clinical treatment recommendation is not supported. This hierarchy prevents directional bulk evidence, a confounded proteomic association or database drug records from being misinterpreted as PBC clinical efficacy.
